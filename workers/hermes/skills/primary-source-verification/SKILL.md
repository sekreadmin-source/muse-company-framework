# Skill: Primary-Source Verification (worker)

Load this skill for any research, scouting, or fact-finding package.

## The rule

A claim in your deliverable must trace to a primary source you actually
opened: the official documentation, the program's own scope page, the
live system itself, the chain, the file. Secondary summaries (articles,
aggregators, other agents' reports, your own prior notes) may *point* to
evidence — they never *are* evidence.

## Practice

- For every load-bearing fact, record in the deliverable: the claim, the
  source (URL or system), and when you observed it.
- When sources disagree, report the disagreement and which source is
  authoritative for the question (the official scope beats a blog post;
  the live system beats the doc).
- Distinguish three tiers explicitly: **verified** (saw it at the primary
  source), **reported** (a credible secondary source says it), **inferred**
  (your reasoning from verified facts). Never let a tier blur upward.
- Prices, deadlines, balances, and statuses are volatile: an old
  observation is a historical note, not a current fact. Re-check at the
  source when the package's decision depends on it.
- If a primary source is unreachable, say so and say what you could not
  verify — an honest gap beats a confident guess, always.
